# Chapter 25: Cosmic Inflation as ψ-Phase Transition

## The Great Expansion

In the universe's first fraction of a second, space expanded faster than light, growing by a factor of 10^26 or more. This cosmic inflation solved fundamental puzzles but raised new questions about its cause. Collapse theory reveals inflation as a phase transition in the ψ-field—a dramatic reorganization from symmetric to broken phase that released enormous energy, driving exponential expansion. Understanding this transition illuminates the universe's birth.

## 25.1 Pre-Inflation Phase

**Definition 25.1** (Symmetric ψ-Phase):
Before inflation, the ψ-field existed in unstable symmetry:
$$V(\psi) = \frac{\lambda}{4}(\psi^2 - v^2)^2$$

with ψ = 0 locally stable but globally unstable.

## 25.2 Phase Transition Dynamics

The transition proceeds through stages:

**Nucleation**: Quantum fluctuations create ψ ≠ 0 bubbles
**Growth**: Bubbles expand at nearly light speed
**Percolation**: Bubbles merge, filling space
**Completion**: New phase dominates everywhere

Timeline: 10^(-35) to 10^(-32) seconds after Big Bang.

## 25.3 Energy Release

**Theorem 25.1** (Vacuum Energy):
Phase transition releases energy density:
$$\rho_{vac} = V(0) - V(v) = \frac{\lambda v^4}{4}$$

This drives exponential expansion:
$$a(t) = a_0 e^{Ht}$$

where H = √(8πGρ_vac/3).

*Proof*:
Friedmann equation with constant ρ gives ä/a = constant. Integration yields exponential solution. ∎

## 25.4 Slow-Roll Conditions

Successful inflation requires:

$$\epsilon = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{V'}{V}\right)^2 < 1$$
$$\eta = \frac{V''}{V} < 1$$

These ensure:
- Sufficient expansion (> 60 e-folds)
- Nearly scale-invariant fluctuations
- Graceful exit to hot Big Bang

## 25.5 Quantum Fluctuations

**Definition 25.2** (Primordial Perturbations):
Quantum ψ-fluctuations during inflation:
$$\langle\delta\psi(k)\delta\psi(k')\rangle = \frac{H^2}{2k^3}(2\pi)^3\delta(k+k')$$

become classical density perturbations post-inflation.

## 25.6 Reheating

After inflation, oscillating ψ decays:

$$\psi(t) = \psi_0 e^{-\Gamma t/2}\cos(mt)$$

Decay products thermalize, creating hot Big Bang:
$$T_{reheat} = \left(\frac{90}{\pi^2 g_*}\right)^{1/4}\sqrt{\Gamma M_P}$$

## 25.7 Multi-Field Inflation

**Theorem 25.2** (Assisted Inflation):
Multiple ψ-fields can drive inflation:
$$H^2 = \frac{8\pi G}{3}\sum_i V_i(\psi_i)$$

Creating richer dynamics and observables.

## 25.8 Eternal Inflation

In some regions, inflation never ends:

**Condition**: Quantum fluctuations > classical roll
**Result**: Fractal universe of pocket universes
**Measure Problem**: How to compare infinities?

Our observable universe is one pocket among infinite many.

## 25.9 Topological Defects

Phase transition can create defects:

**Monopoles**: Point defects (inflated away)
**Strings**: Line defects (possibly observable)
**Walls**: Surface defects (domain boundaries)
**Textures**: 3D defects (unstable)

Defect density depends on transition details.

## 25.10 Observable Signatures

Inflation's ψ-transition leaves marks:

$$n_s = 1 - 6\epsilon + 2\eta \approx 0.96$$
$$r = 16\epsilon < 0.06$$

where n_s is scalar spectral index, r is tensor-to-scalar ratio.

## 25.11 Alternative Scenarios

Other ψ-transitions could drive inflation:

**Hybrid Inflation**: Two-field transition
**Chaotic Inflation**: Large-field evolution
**Natural Inflation**: Periodic potential
**Brane Inflation**: Higher-dimensional transition

Each predicts distinct observables.

**Principle 25.1** (Inflation Universality):
Any ψ-phase transition with:
- Sufficient potential energy

generates inflation-like expansion.

Our universe's homogeneity witnesses ancient phase transition.
