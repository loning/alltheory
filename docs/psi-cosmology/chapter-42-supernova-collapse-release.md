# Chapter 42: Supernova - Collapse Release Explosions

## 42.1 Cosmic Fireworks: Collapse Unleashed

Supernovae represent the universe's most energetic events—moments when gravitational collapse catastrophically reverses, launching stellar material at significant fractions of light speed. These explosions shape galactic evolution, forge heavy elements, and trigger new star formation.

**Definition 42.1** (Supernova Energy Scale): A typical supernova releases:
$$E_{SN} ≈ 10^{51} erg = 10^{44} J ≈ M__\odotc² × 10^{-3}$$

Energy partition:
- Kinetic energy: ~10⁵¹ erg (99%)
- Photon energy: ~10⁴⁹ erg (1%)
- Neutrino energy: ~10⁵³ erg (99% escapes)
- Gravitational waves: ~10⁴⁸ erg

## 42.2 Core Collapse Mechanism: Implosion to Explosion

The transition from collapse to explosion remains one of astrophysics' grand challenges:

**Theorem 42.1** (Collapse Instability): Core collapse initiates when:
$$\frac{|E_{grav}|}{E_{thermal} + E_{degeneracy}} > 1 + ε_Ψ$$

where ε_Ψ accounts for nuclear binding energy changes.

*Proof*: From virial theorem including nuclear energy... ∎

## 42.3 Neutrino Physics: Invisible Energy Carriers

Neutrinos dominate supernova energetics:

**Definition 42.2** (Neutrino Luminosity):
$$L_ν = 4πR_ν² × σT_ν⁴ × \frac{c}{λ_ν}$$

where:
- R_ν ≈ 10 km (neutrinosphere radius)
- T_ν ≈ 4 MeV (neutrino temperature)
- λ_ν = mean free path

The neutrino flux creates conditions for:
$$Heating > Cooling$$ 
in the gain region, driving explosion.

## 42.4 Shock Revival: The Critical Transition

The stalled shock must be revived for successful explosion:

**Theorem 42.2** (Revival Criterion): Explosion occurs when:
$$τ_\{adv\} × Q_ν > |E_\{bind\}|$$

where:
- τ_\{adv\} = advection time through gain region
- Q_ν = neutrino heating rate
- E_\{bind\} = binding energy above shock

Multi-dimensional effects enhance revival:
- Convection (increases τ_\{adv\})
- SASI (standing accretion shock instability)
- Turbulence (improves heating efficiency)

## 42.5 Nucleosynthesis: Element Factory

Supernovae synthesize elements through multiple processes:

**Definition 42.3** (Nucleosynthesis Zones):
1. **Silicon burning**: Complete to iron peak
2. **Alpha-rich freeze-out**: ⁴⁴Ti, ⁴⁸Cr, ⁵²Fe
3. **Explosive burning**: O, Ne, C shells
4. **Neutrino process**: ⁷Li, ¹¹B, ¹⁹F
5. **r-process**: Neutron-rich heavy elements

Yields depend on:
$$Y_i = f(M_*, Z_*, E_{explosion}, Ψ_{asymmetry})$$

## 42.6 Light Curves: Collapse Afterglow

Supernova brightness evolution reveals explosion physics:

**Theorem 42.3** (Bolometric Light Curve): Luminosity follows:
$$L(t) = \frac{E_\{dep\}}{t} × e^{-t/τ} + L_\{radioactive\}(t)$$

where:
- E_\{dep\} = deposited kinetic energy
- τ = photon diffusion time
- L_\{radioactive\} = ⁵⁶Ni → ⁵⁶Co → ⁵⁶Fe decay

Peak luminosity:
$$L_{peak} ≈ 10^{43} erg/s ≈ 10^9 L__\odot$$

## 42.7 Supernova Classification: Collapse Varieties

Different progenitors produce distinct supernova types:

**Definition 42.4** (Spectroscopic Classification):
- **Type II**: H lines present (massive star collapse)
  - II-P: Plateau light curve
  - II-L: Linear decline
  - IIb: H lines disappear
  - IIn: Narrow emission lines
- **Type I**: No H lines
  - Ia: Si II absorption (thermonuclear)
  - Ib: He lines, no H (stripped envelope)
  - Ic: No He or H (highly stripped)

## 42.8 Type Ia: Thermonuclear Explosions

White dwarf explosions serve as standard candles:

**Theorem 42.4** (Chandrasekhar Ignition): Explosion triggers when:
$$M_{WD} → M_{Ch}$$ via accretion or merger

Carbon fusion runaway:
$$^{12}C + ^{12}C → ^{24}Mg^* → products$$

proceeds supersonically, disrupting entire star.

Standardizable luminosity:
$$M_B^{peak} = -19.3 ± 0.3$$ 
enables cosmological distance measurements.

## 42.9 Supernova Remnants: Collapse Echoes

Post-explosion evolution creates expanding shells:

**Definition 42.5** (Remnant Evolution Phases):
1. **Free expansion**: v = constant, R ∝ t
2. **Sedov-Taylor**: Self-similar blast wave
3. **Radiative**: Cooling becomes important
4. **Momentum-conserving**: Merges with ISM

Shell dynamics:
$$\frac{dR}{dt} = v_{shock} = \sqrt{\frac{2E}{πρR³}}$$

## 42.10 Cosmic Ray Acceleration: Collapse Energy Conversion

Supernova shocks accelerate particles to extreme energies:

**Theorem 42.5** (Fermi Acceleration): Maximum energy:
$$E_{max} ≈ Ze × B × R_{shock} × β_{shock}$$

where:
- Z = atomic number
- B = magnetic field
- R_{shock} = shock radius
- β_{shock} = v/c

This process converts ~10% of explosion energy into cosmic rays.

## 42.11 Compact Remnants: Collapse Endpoints

Explosions leave behind neutron stars or black holes:

**Definition 42.6** (Remnant Mass Function):
$$M_{remnant} = f(M_{ZAMS}, Z, Ψ_{rotation}, binary)$$

Approximate boundaries:
- M < 8 M__\odot: White dwarf
- 8 < M < 25 M__\odot: Neutron star
- M > 25 M__\odot: Black hole
- (with significant uncertainty and dependence on physics)

## 42.12 Supernova Feedback: Galactic Regulation

Explosions inject energy and metals into surroundings:

**Theorem 42.6** (Feedback Efficiency): Energy coupling:
$$E_{coupled} = ε_{SN} × E_{SN} × f(ρ_{ISM}, metallicity)$$

Effects include:
- ISM heating and ionization
- Turbulence driving
- Outflow launching
- Star formation triggering/suppression

## 42.13 Pair Instability: Extreme Collapse

Very massive stars experience pair-production instability:

**Definition 42.7** (Pair Instability): When T > 10⁹ K:
$$γ + γ → e^+ + e^-$$

reduces radiation pressure, triggering collapse.

Results:
- 100 < M < 130 M__\odot: Pulsational mass loss
- 130 < M < 250 M__\odot: Complete disruption
- M > 250 M__\odot: Direct black hole formation

## 42.14 Magnetar Formation: Extreme Fields

Some supernovae produce magnetars with B ~ 10¹⁵ G:

**Theorem 42.7** (Magnetic Field Amplification):
$$B_{final} = B_{initial} × \left(\frac{R_{initial}}{R_{final}}\right)² × A_{dynamo}$$

where A_{dynamo} >> 1 from differential rotation and convection.

Magnetar properties:
- Spin-down dominated by magnetic dipole
- Giant flares from magnetic reconnection
- Possible GRB connection

## 42.15 Multi-Messenger Events: Collapse Signals

Modern astronomy detects supernovae across wavebands:

**Definition 42.8** (Multi-Messenger Signatures):
1. **Neutrinos**: ~10 s burst before optical
2. **Gravitational waves**: Core bounce signal
3. **Electromagnetic**: Radio to gamma rays
4. **Cosmic rays**: Delayed by diffusion

Combined observations reveal:
- Explosion asymmetry
- Progenitor properties
- Nuclear physics
- Fundamental particle properties

## 42.16 Death and Transfiguration

Supernovae embody cosmic creative destruction—stellar death enabling galactic rebirth. Each explosion scatters elements forged over millions of years, enriching space with the raw materials for new worlds. In their violent end, stars achieve immortality.

**The Forty-Second Echo**: "In the moment of ultimate collapse, a star's death becomes the universe's gain—explosion reversing implosion, scattering stardust that will one day think and dream and wonder at its own cosmic origins."
