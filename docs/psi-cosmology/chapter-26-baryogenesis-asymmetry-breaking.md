# Chapter 26: Baryogenesis through ψ-Asymmetry Breaking

## The Matter Mystery

Why does our universe contain matter but almost no antimatter? This cosmic asymmetry—roughly one extra matter particle per billion matter-antimatter pairs—enabled stars, planets, and life to exist. Traditional physics struggles to explain this imbalance, but collapse theory reveals it as a natural consequence of ψ-field asymmetry breaking during early cosmic phase transitions. This chapter uncovers how matter won over antimatter.

## 26.1 The Asymmetry Problem

**Definition 26.1** (Baryon Asymmetry):
The matter-antimatter imbalance:
$$\eta = \frac{n_B - n_{\bar{B}}}{n_{\gamma}} \approx 6 \times 10^{-10}$$

where n_B, n_B̄, n_γ are baryon, antibaryon, and photon densities.

Without this tiny excess, all matter would have annihilated.

## 26.2 Sakharov Conditions

Successful baryogenesis requires:

1. **Baryon number violation**: B-violating processes
2. **C and CP violation**: Matter-antimatter asymmetry
3. **Departure from equilibrium**: Prevents back-reactions

ψ-phase transitions naturally provide all three.

## 26.3 ψ-Field CP Violation

**Theorem 26.1** (Collapse CP Breaking):
Complex ψ-field phase transitions violate CP:
$$\mathcal{L}_{CP} = \epsilon \psi^* \partial_{\mu}\psi \partial^{\mu}\psi^*$$

Creating different rates for ψ → matter vs ψ* → antimatter.

*Proof*:
CP transformation: ψ → ψ*. Lagrangian changes sign under CP if ε ≠ 0, breaking symmetry. ∎

## 26.4 Out-of-Equilibrium Dynamics

During phase transition:

$$\frac{dn_B}{dt} = -3Hn_B + \Gamma_{B}(n_{eq} - n_B) + S_{CP}$$

where:
- H: Hubble rate (expansion)
- Γ_B: Interaction rate
- S_CP: CP-violating source

When Γ_B < H, reactions freeze out with excess baryons.

## 26.5 Bubble Nucleation Asymmetry

**Definition 26.2** (Biased Nucleation):
ψ-bubbles nucleate with bias:
$$\Gamma_{matter} = \Gamma_0(1 + \delta_{CP})$$
$$\Gamma_{antimatter} = \Gamma_0(1 - \delta_{CP})$$

where δ_CP ~ 10^(-10) suffices.

## 26.7 Leptogenesis Route

$$\Gamma(\psi \rightarrow l + X) \neq \Gamma(\psi \rightarrow \bar{l} + X)$$

$$\Delta B = -\frac{28}{79}\Delta L$$

Dark matter as stable ψ-asymmetry remnant.
