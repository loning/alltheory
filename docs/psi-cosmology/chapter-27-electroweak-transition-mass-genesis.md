# Chapter 27: Electroweak Transition and Mass Genesis

## The Birth of Mass

At a cosmic age of 10^(-12) seconds, when the universe cooled to 100 GeV, a remarkable transformation occurred: particles that had been massless suddenly acquired mass through the electroweak phase transition. This mechanism, involving the Higgs field, fundamentally altered particle interactions and made atoms possible. Collapse theory reveals this transition as a ψ-field reorganization that broke electroweak symmetry and generated the mass spectrum we observe.

## 27.1 Electroweak Unification

**Definition 27.1** (Unified Phase):
Above critical temperature T_c ≈ 160 GeV:
$$\mathcal{L}_{EW} = -\frac{1}{4}W_{\mu\nu}^aW^{a\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{4}B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}$$

W and Z bosons massless, electromagnetic and weak forces unified.

## 27.2 Higgs-ψ Coupling

The Higgs field couples to collapse:

$$V(\phi, \psi) = \lambda_{\phi}(\phi^{\dagger}\phi - v^2)^2 + g\psi^2\phi^{\dagger}\phi$$

where φ is Higgs doublet, ψ drives transition.

## 27.3 Symmetry Breaking

**Theorem 27.1** (Mass Generation):
When T < T_c, Higgs develops VEV:
$$\langle\phi\rangle = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ v/\sqrt{2} \end{pmatrix}$$

Generating masses:
$$M_W = \frac{gv}{2}, \quad M_Z = \frac{gv}{2\cos\theta_W}$$

*Proof*:
Minimize potential V(φ,ψ). Non-zero minimum breaks SU(2)×U(1) → U(1)_EM. Gauge boson masses arise from covariant derivatives. ∎

## 27.4 Fermion Mass Hierarchy

Yukawa couplings generate fermion masses:

$$m_f = y_f \frac{v}{\sqrt{2}}$$

Mass hierarchy from ψ-dependent couplings:
$$y_f = y_0 \exp(-\lambda_f/\psi)$$

Explaining why m_top >> m_electron.
